Legal Framework

What Is an Industrial Design?

Under Article 119 of Law No. 82 of 2002, an industrial design is defined as any composition of lines or any three-dimensional form — with or without colors — that gives a special appearance of novelty and is industrially applicable.

Requirements for Protection

The design must be new (not disclosed to the public before filing), have individual character, and be industrially applicable.

What Cannot Be Registered

Under Article 124: designs dictated solely by technical or functional considerations; designs containing national emblems, religious symbols, or flags; designs contrary to public order or morality.

Protection Term

10 years from the filing date — renewable once for a further 5 years, giving a maximum total of 15 years.

Official Fees

Under Article 122, the total official fees for design applications, renewals, and related procedures shall not exceed EGP 3,000.

Opposition Period

Any interested party may oppose a design registration within 60 days from the date of publication in the Official Gazette.

Priority Right

Under the Paris Convention, applicants may claim priority within 6 months from the first filing in a member state.

What can be protected?

Industrial designs protect the appearance of a product — not its technical function. Examples include: packaging and containers, furniture and household goods, lighting equipment, jewelry, electronic devices, textiles, and vehicle body parts. The design must be visible in the finished product.

  • Electronic devices and appliances

  • Vehicle body parts and components
  • Toys and sporting equipment
  • Industrial equipment and machinery
  • Building components (bricks, tiles, panels)
  • Kitchenware and tableware
  • Cosmetics and personal care packaging
