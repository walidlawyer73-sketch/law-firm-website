Civil Cases in Egypt

Types of Civil Disputes

Under Egyptian Civil Law, civil disputes span several categories. Here are the most common types we handle.

Contractual Disputes

Disputes over the formation, performance, or termination of contracts — including sale, lease, services, and construction contracts.

Tort Claims

Claims based on civil liability — negligence, defective products, professional malpractice, and personal injury.

Property & Ownership

Ownership claims, adverse possession, easements, and boundary disputes over immovable property.

Debt & Loan Recovery

Enforcement of payment obligations, loan defaults, and recovery of debts owed by individuals or entities.

Family Property

Civil aspects of family law — inheritance claims, division of inherited assets, and matrimonial property.

Insurance Disputes

Claims against insurers for refused or delayed payments — under life, property, or liability policies.

Note on civil procedure

Civil cases in Egypt are governed by the Civil and Commercial Procedure Code (Law No. 13 of 1968). Under the 2024 amendments (Law No. 157 of 2024), the monetary jurisdiction of the District Courts was raised to EGP 200,000, with judgments final where the claim does not exceed EGP 30,000. The Court of Cassation now reviews claims exceeding EGP 500,000. These thresholds affect where your case is filed and what appeal routes are available.

Common Questions About Civil Litigation

Civil litigation deals with disputes between private parties — contracts, torts, property, and family property matters. Commercial litigation is a subset dealing with disputes arising from commercial activities and governed by commercial law — including commercial contracts, agency, distribution, and corporate disputes.

Timelines vary by court and complexity. First-instance proceedings typically take 12–24 months; appeals may extend the process by another 12–18 months. Cassation review adds further time. We provide realistic timelines at the outset and keep clients updated throughout.

Evidence typically includes written documents (contracts, correspondence, receipts), witness testimony, expert reports (in technical or financial matters), and — where necessary — evidence from court-appointed experts. Under the Civil and Commercial Procedure Code, written evidence plays a central role in most civil cases.

Enforcement of Egyptian judgments abroad depends on the existence of a bilateral or multilateral enforcement treaty between Egypt and the target jurisdiction, or on reciprocity. In some cases, a new action may be required in the foreign court. Where enforcement abroad is anticipated, we recommend including an arbitration clause in the underlying contract, as arbitral awards are enforceable in over 170 countries under the New York Convention.
