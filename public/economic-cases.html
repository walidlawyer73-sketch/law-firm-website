Judicial Structure

Egypt's Economic Courts

Established by Law No. 120 of 2008, as amended — most recently by Law No. 156 of 2024 — the Economic Courts consist of two circuits within each Economic Court.

First Instance

First Instance Circuit

الدائرة الابتدائية

  • Handles claims valued at up to EGP 15 million
  • Judgment is final if the claim value does not exceed EGP 750,000
  • Composed of three judges from the Courts of First Instance
  • Also hears misdemeanor cases arising from the 21 laws within its jurisdiction
Best for Small and medium-sized commercial disputes, IP infringement claims, and regulatory matters below the EGP 15 million threshold.
Appellate

Appellate Circuit

الدائرة الاستئنافية

  • Handles claims valued at more than EGP 15 million
  • Also handles claims of undetermined value
  • Hears appeals against First Instance Circuit judgments
  • Composed of three counsellors from the Courts of Appeal
Best for Large commercial disputes, complex IP litigation, competition cases, and matters of significant economic value.

Note on the 2024 amendments

Under Law No. 156 of 2024 (amending the Economic Courts Law), the monetary jurisdiction of the First Instance Circuit was raised to EGP 15 million (previously EGP 5 million under Law No. 191 of 2020). The finality threshold was set at EGP 750,000. The Appellate Circuit now handles claims exceeding EGP 15 million or of undetermined value. Appeals against Appellate Circuit judgments — and against First Instance Circuit judgments where the value exceeds EGP 750,000 — go to the Court of Cassation within 40 days of the judgment.

Why the Economic Courts matter

The Economic Courts were established to provide a specialized, expedited judicial track for commercial and economic disputes — with judges experienced in economic matters, streamlined procedures, and a unified jurisdiction. For businesses operating in Egypt, they offer a more efficient path to resolving complex commercial and IP disputes than the ordinary civil courts.

Common Questions About Economic Courts

The Economic Courts were established by Law No. 120 of 2008 to provide a specialized judicial track for economic and commercial disputes. They have exclusive jurisdiction over disputes arising from 21 laws — including intellectual property, competition, capital markets, companies, investment, and banking. The courts were designed to provide faster, more specialized resolution of complex economic matters.

Under the 2024 amendments (Law No. 156 of 2024): The First Instance Circuit handles claims valued at up to EGP 15 million, with judgments final where the claim does not exceed EGP 750,000. The Appellate Circuit handles claims exceeding EGP 15 million or of undetermined value. Appeals against Appellate Circuit judgments go to the Court of Cassation within 40 days.

The full list is set out in Article 4 of the Economic Courts Law.

The "Golden List" is a register of experts maintained by the Economic Courts.
