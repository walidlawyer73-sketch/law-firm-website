Judicial Structure

Egypt's State Council

Under Article 172 of the Constitution and Law No. 47 of 1972, the State Council is an independent judicial body with exclusive jurisdiction over administrative disputes and disciplinary cases.

Supreme Administrative Court

The highest court within the State Council. It reviews appeals against judgments of the Administrative Judiciary Court and the Disciplinary Courts — on grounds of legal error. Composed of panels of five judges.

Administrative Judiciary Court

The principal court for administrative disputes. It has jurisdiction over annulment claims, compensation claims, and administrative contract disputes. Judgments issued by panels of three counsellors.

Administrative Courts

Handle certain categories of administrative disputes as defined by law — including specific local and regional matters.

Disciplinary Courts

Hear disciplinary cases involving public officials, judges, and members of certain professions. They adjudicate alleged violations of professional and administrative duties.

State Commissioners Authority

The advisory body within the State Council. It prepares the legal opinion for the court in each case — and its members participate in hearings before the Administrative Judiciary Court and Disciplinary Courts.

Advisory & Legislative Sections

Beyond the judiciary, the State Council includes the Fatwa (Advisory) Section and the Legislation Section — providing legal opinions to the Government and reviewing draft laws and contracts.

Note on jurisdiction

Under Article 10 of Law No. 47 of 1972, the State Council courts have exclusive jurisdiction over a wide range of administrative matters — including annulment of final administrative decisions, compensation claims, public employment disputes, local elections, administrative contracts, and disciplinary cases. This jurisdiction is exclusive: ordinary courts cannot hear these disputes.

FAQ

Common Questions About State Council Cases

The State Council is an independent judicial body established under Article 172 of the Egyptian Constitution. It has exclusive jurisdiction over administrative disputes — meaning disputes between individuals or companies and government entities. It is the guarantor of the principle of legality in administrative action and the protector of citizens' rights against unlawful administrative decisions.

Under Article 10 of Law No. 47 of 1972, the State Council courts hear: annulment claims against final administrative decisions; compensation claims arising from such decisions; disputes over salaries, pensions, and benefits for public officials; appointment and promotion disputes; local council election disputes; administrative contract disputes; disciplinary cases; and other administrative disputes as defined by law.

An annulment claim seeks to cancel an unlawful administrative decision — restoring the legal position to what it was before the decision was issued. A compensation claim seeks monetary compensation for the harm caused by the unlawful decision. The two can be filed together — with the compensation claim either original or incidental to the annulment claim.

Timelines vary depending on the court and complexity. First-instance proceedings before the Administrative Judiciary Court typically take 12–24 months; appeals before the Supreme Administrative Court can extend the process by another 12–18 months. The State Commissioners' report phase adds further time. We provide realistic timelines at the outset.

The court may order suspension if the decision is likely unlawful and execution would cause serious harm. The suspension request is heard on an expedited basis.
