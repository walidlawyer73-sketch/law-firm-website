Arbitration Trends to Watch in 2026: Egypt and the Region

Walid Mohamed Abu Al-Ela February 2026 7 min read

Arbitration in Egypt and the wider MENA region is evolving rapidly. Regulatory reforms, the rise of IP disputes, the growing sophistication of regional arbitral institutions, and the impact of technology are all reshaping how disputes are resolved. Here are the trends that matter most in 2026.

In This Article

  1. The Regional Landscape
  2. Key Trends for 2026
  3. IP Arbitration: A Growing Niche
  4. Investment Arbitration
  5. The Role of Technology

The Regional Landscape

Egypt has long been a significant player in regional arbitration — thanks to its central geographic position, its established legal infrastructure, and the presence of institutions such as the Cairo Regional Centre for International Commercial Arbitration (CRCICA). Over the past decade, the region as a whole has seen a steady increase in the use of arbitration for cross-border disputes.

This growth has been driven by several factors:

  • Increased foreign investment — more cross-border transactions mean more disputes governed by arbitration clauses;
  • Legal reforms — many MENA jurisdictions have updated their arbitration laws to align with the UNCITRAL Model Law;
  • Regional institutions — arbitral centres in Cairo, Dubai, Riyadh, and elsewhere have grown in prominence and professionalism;
  • Enforcement improvements — the New York Convention continues to be the backbone of cross-border enforcement, with gradual improvements in regional implementation.

Key Trends for 2026

Based on current practice, we identify the following six trends as particularly important:

01

IP Disputes on the Rise

Arbitration of intellectual property disputes is becoming more common, especially in licensing, franchising, and technology transfer.

02

Cross-Border Investment Cases

Investor-State arbitration remains active, with disputes arising from regulatory changes and contract breaches.

03

Technology in Arbitration

Virtual hearings, e-discovery, and AI-assisted case management are becoming standard practice.

04

Diversity & Regional Talent

Growing demand for regionally qualified arbitrators — including bilingual Arabic-English practitioners.

05

Third-Party Funding

Third-party funding continues to expand, with evolving regulatory frameworks across jurisdictions.

06

Accelerated Procedures

More institutions are offering expedited procedures for smaller or time-sensitive disputes.

IP Arbitration: A Growing Niche

One of the most notable developments is the growth of IP arbitration. Historically, IP disputes were handled primarily through national courts — because they often involve registrations, validity determinations, and public policy questions. But arbitration offers advantages that are becoming increasingly attractive:

  • Confidentiality — crucial for protecting trade secrets, proprietary technology, and business relationships;
  • Speed — often faster than multi-jurisdictional litigation;
  • Specialized expertise — parties can choose arbitrators with deep IP knowledge;
  • Cross-border enforcement — the New York Convention facilitates enforcement across multiple jurisdictions;
  • Flexibility — parties can tailor procedures to the specific needs of their dispute.

In Egypt specifically, the National Institute of Intellectual Property has established a specialized center for IP arbitration and mediation, reflecting the growing recognition that IP disputes require specialist resolution mechanisms.

What this means for businesses

If your contracts involve IP licensing, technology transfer, or franchising in the region, consider including arbitration clauses that specify IP-experienced arbitrators and appropriate institutional rules. This can save significant time and cost when disputes arise.

Investment Arbitration

Investment arbitration — typically under bilateral investment treaties (BITs) or the ICSID Convention — continues to be a significant area of practice in the MENA region. Egypt is party to a number of BITs and has been involved in several investor-State cases over the past decade.

Key trends in investment arbitration include:

  • Regulatory changes — economic reforms can give rise to investment disputes when they affect existing rights;
  • Contract-based claims — many investment disputes arise from contracts rather than treaty breaches;
  • State-owned entities — cases involving state-owned enterprises remain common;
  • Settlement incentives — an increasing number of cases are resolved through negotiated settlement before final award.

For foreign investors in Egypt — or Egyptian investors abroad — understanding the protections available under applicable BITs and investment contracts is essential.

Investment arbitration is not simply a matter of treaty interpretation. It requires strategic understanding of the political, regulatory, and commercial context in which investments operate.

The Role of Technology

Technology is transforming arbitration practice across the region. The most significant developments include:

5.1 Virtual and Hybrid Hearings

The shift to virtual hearings — accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic — has become permanent. Most major institutions now offer hybrid and virtual hearing options as standard, reducing travel costs and enabling participation from multiple locations.

5.2 E-Discovery and Document Management

The volume of documents in complex arbitrations has grown dramatically. Modern case management platforms facilitate efficient disclosure, review, and presentation of evidence — with AI assistance increasingly used for document classification and pattern detection.

5.3 AI-Assisted Case Analysis

AI tools are being used for legal research, precedent analysis, and even initial drafting of procedural submissions. This raises important questions about quality control, confidentiality, and professional responsibility.

5.4 Cybersecurity

As arbitrations become more digital, cybersecurity becomes a critical concern. Best practice now includes secure platforms, robust access controls, and clear confidentiality protocols for both parties and tribunals.

Practical Implications

What do these trends mean for businesses operating in Egypt and the region?

✓ Review Your Dispute Resolution Clauses

Make sure your contracts include clear, enforceable arbitration clauses. Consider:

  • The seat of arbitration (and why it matters);
  • The institutional rules you want to apply;
  • The language of the proceedings;
  • The number and qualifications of arbitrators.

✓ Consider Specialist Experience

For IP, technology, or investment disputes, choose arbitrators and counsel with relevant expertise. Generic arbitration experience is not a substitute for sector knowledge.

✓ Plan for Evidence Early

Document management and evidence preservation need to be addressed from the outset. Digital evidence requirements and confidentiality protections should be anticipated.

✓ Explore Settlement Where Appropriate

Arbitration is a means, not an end. Mediation and negotiated settlement are often more efficient — and can preserve commercial relationships that litigation or arbitration might damage.

Conclusion

Arbitration in Egypt and the MENA region is in a phase of significant evolution. IP arbitration is growing, investment cases remain significant, technology is changing practice, and regional institutions are becoming more sophisticated.

For businesses operating in the region, the message is straightforward: a strong arbitration strategy is no longer a "nice to have" — it's a core part of contractual risk management.

The trends we've outlined are not speculative. They are already shaping how disputes are handled today. Businesses that understand them — and adapt their contracts, procedures, and strategies accordingly — will be better positioned to protect their interests when disputes arise.
