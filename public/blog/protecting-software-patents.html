Protecting Software in Egypt: Copyright, Patents, or Trade Secrets?

Walid Mohamed Abu Al-Ela February 2026 8 min read

Software is one of the most valuable — and most difficult to protect — forms of intellectual property. In Egypt, developers, SaaS founders, and tech companies have three main legal paths available: copyright, patents, and trade secrets. Each offers different protections, different costs, and different strategic value. This guide breaks down when and how to use each.

Why Software Protection Is Unique

Software is unusual in the intellectual property world because it combines several different types of creative and technical output in a single product:

  • Source code — which is a form of written expression;
  • User interface — which has visual and design elements;
  • Algorithms and methods — which are functional and technical;
  • Data structures and architecture — which may be novel or inventive;
  • Business logic and processes — which may constitute trade secrets.

Because of this complexity, no single legal tool covers all aspects of software. A well-designed IP strategy typically combines multiple forms of protection — each addressing a different layer of the product.

Path 1: Copyright

Copyright is the most common — and most accessible — form of software protection in Egypt. Under Law No. 82 of 2002 on the Protection of Intellectual Property Rights, computer programs are explicitly protected as literary works.

What Copyright Protects

  • The source code — as originally written;
  • The object code — the compiled version;
  • The structure, sequence, and organization of the program, in certain cases;
  • The user interface elements — where they constitute original expression.

What Copyright Does NOT Protect

  • Ideas, algorithms, or methods — as such;
  • Functional aspects of the software;
  • Business logic — unless expressed in a protected form.

Key Advantages

  • Automatic — protection arises upon creation, no registration required (though registration is available);
  • Long duration — protection lasts for the life of the author plus 50 years in Egypt;
  • Low cost — no formal fees required to obtain protection;
  • International recognition — protected in most countries via the Berne Convention.

Key Limitations

  • Protects expression, not function — a competitor can write different code that performs the same function;
  • Difficult to enforce against reverse engineering in some cases;
  • Does not protect the underlying idea or method.

Practical tip for developers

Even though copyright is automatic, consider registering your software with the Egyptian Patent Office / relevant authority. Registration provides a formal record of authorship, which can be valuable evidence if you ever need to enforce your rights in court.

Path 2: Patents

Patents protect inventions — new, non-obvious, and industrially applicable technical solutions. In some jurisdictions, software-related inventions can be patented if they meet these criteria. In Egypt, patenting software is more complex.

Software Patents in Egypt

Under Egyptian law, "computer programs" as such are excluded from patentability. However, this exclusion does not necessarily extend to inventions that use software to solve a technical problem — for example:

  • A novel algorithm applied to a specific technical process (such as image processing or encryption);
  • A hardware-software combination that solves a technical problem in a new way;
  • A method that produces a technical effect beyond normal computer operation.

In practice, obtaining a software-related patent in Egypt requires careful drafting to position the invention as a technical solution, not merely as a computer program. This is a specialist task that should be handled by a registered patent agent.

Key Advantages

  • Strong protection — protects the underlying invention, not just the expression;
  • Excludes competitors — others cannot use the patented method, even with different code;
  • Commercial value — patents are valuable assets for licensing and investment.

Key Limitations

  • High cost — patent filing is expensive, and prosecution can take years;
  • Disclosure requirement — a patent must publicly disclose the invention, ending secrecy;
  • Limited term — 20 years from filing, then protection expires;
  • Hard to obtain — software inventions face stricter examination.

Path 3: Trade Secrets

Trade secrets protect confidential business information that gives its owner a competitive advantage — including software source code, algorithms, and technical know-how.

In Egypt, trade secrets are protected under Law No. 82 of 2002 and by contract (through NDAs and confidentiality clauses). There is no formal registration process — protection arises from maintaining secrecy.

What Trade Secrets Protect

  • Source code that is not publicly disclosed;
  • Proprietary algorithms and business logic;
  • Technical know-how — including methods, processes, and experience;
  • Customer data, pricing models, and business strategies.

Key Advantages

  • No registration cost — protection is free, provided secrecy is maintained;
  • No disclosure requirement — you do not need to reveal how your technology works;
  • Unlimited duration — protection lasts as long as secrecy is maintained (think of the Coca-Cola formula);
  • Broad scope — can protect things that copyright and patents cannot.

Key Limitations

  • Requires strict confidentiality — protection is lost if the secret becomes public;
  • Vulnerable to reverse engineering — if a competitor can legally discover the secret from your product, protection is lost;
  • Enforcement challenges — proving misappropriation can be difficult;
  • Employee risk — requires strong internal controls and exit procedures.

Trade secret protection is only as strong as the discipline behind it. A perfect confidentiality clause is useless without the operational practice to enforce it.

Side-by-Side Comparison

The following table summarizes the key differences between the three paths:

Factor Copyright Patent Trade Secret
What it protects Expression (code, UI) Technical invention Confidential information
Registration Automatic (optional) Required Not required (secrecy)
Cost Low High Low (but operational cost)
Duration Life + 50 years (Egypt) 20 years As long as secret is maintained
Disclosure Not required Required Must remain secret
Protects function? No Yes Yes (if kept secret)
Enforcement difficulty Moderate Moderate-High High

Building a Layered Strategy

The most effective approach for most software companies is a layered strategy — combining multiple forms of protection to cover different aspects of the product. Here's a practical framework:

Layer 1: Copyright (Always)

  • Protect your source code and UI as original expression;
  • Register the software for additional evidence of authorship;
  • Include clear copyright notices in your code and documentation.

Layer 2: Trade Secrets (Almost Always)

  • Keep proprietary algorithms and business logic confidential;
  • Use NDAs with employees, contractors, and partners;
  • Implement access controls and clear exit procedures;
  • Document your confidentiality practices.

Layer 3: Patents (When Applicable)

  • Consider patent protection for genuinely novel technical solutions;
  • Work with a registered patent agent to assess patentability;
  • Balance the cost and disclosure requirements against the strategic value;
  • Consider international filings where commercial value justifies it.

Layer 4: Contracts

  • Use strong end-user license agreements (EULAs);
  • Include anti-reverse-engineering clauses where enforceable;
  • Protect against unauthorized use in SaaS terms of service;
  • Address IP ownership clearly in development agreements.

The layered principle

No single form of protection is sufficient for software. The strongest position comes from combining copyright, trade secrets, patents (where available), and contracts — each covering what the others cannot.

Conclusion

Software protection in Egypt is not a matter of choosing one legal tool — it's about building a strategy that covers the multiple dimensions of your product. Copyright protects your expression, trade secrets protect your know-how, patents protect your technical inventions, and contracts tie it all together.

For developers, SaaS founders, and tech companies, the practical implications are:

  • Start early — protection should be built into development, not added after;
  • Document everything — ownership records, confidentiality practices, and authorship evidence matter;
  • Consult a specialist — the interplay of copyright, patents, and trade secrets requires professional guidance;
  • Think internationally — if your product operates globally, your IP strategy should reflect that.

Software is a strategic asset. Protecting it properly is not an expense — it's an investment in your competitive position.
