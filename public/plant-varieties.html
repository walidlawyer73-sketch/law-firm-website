What Can Be Protected

Eligible Plant Varieties

Protection is available for new plant varieties developed in Egypt or abroad.

  • Field crops (wheat, rice, cotton, maize)
  • Vegetables (tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers)
  • Fruits (citrus, mangoes, grapes, figs)
  • Ornamental plants and flowers
  • Medicinal and aromatic plants
  • Trees and vines (25 years protection)
  • Other agricultural crops (20 years protection)
  • Varieties developed through traditional breeding
  • Varieties developed through biotechnology
  • Varieties developed inside or outside Egypt

Egypt's accession to UPOV

Egypt became a member of the International Union for the Protection of New Varieties of Plants (UPOV) in 2019. The Egyptian plant variety protection regime under Law No. 82 of 2002 aligns with the 1991 UPOV Convention.

Legal Framework

Protection Under Egyptian Law

Plant variety rights in Egypt are governed by Law No. 82 of 2002 (Book Four).

Key legal provisions

Plant variety protection is governed by Book Four of Law No. 82 of 2002 on the Protection of Intellectual Property Rights, as amended by Law No. 26 of 2015. Key provisions include:

Article 189

A variety must be new — not commercialized in Egypt before the filing date, or abroad for more than 6 years (trees/vines) or 4 years (other crops).

Article 190

The variety must be distinct, uniform, and stable (DUS). It must also have a suitable denomination (variety name).

Article 193

Protection lasts for 25 years for trees and vines, and 20 years for other crops — starting from the date the protection certificate is granted.

Article 194

The breeder's right extends to the production, reproduction, conditioning, offering for sale, selling, exporting, importing, and stocking of the protected variety.

Article 195

The breeder's right does not extend to acts done privately for non-commercial purposes, experimental purposes, or for breeding new varieties.

Farmers' Privilege

Under Article 195, farmers may use, save, and replant seeds from protected varieties on their own land, subject to conditions set by the Minister of Agriculture.

FAQ

Common Questions About Plant Varieties

Plant variety protection is an intellectual property right granted to breeders of new plant varieties. In Egypt, it is protected under Law No. 82 of 2002 (Book Four, Articles 189–199). The right gives the breeder exclusive control over the commercial exploitation of the variety.

A variety must meet five conditions: (1) Novelty — not commercialized before the filing date; (2) Distinctness — clearly distinguishable from existing varieties; (3) Uniformity — sufficiently uniform in its relevant characteristics; (4) Stability — remains unchanged after repeated propagation; and (5) a suitable denomination (variety name).

Protection lasts for 25 years for trees and vines, and 20 years for other crops (Article 193). The term runs from the date the protection certificate is granted — not from the filing date.

The DUS test examines three criteria: Distinctness, Uniformity, and Stability. In Egypt, the test is conducted over a two-year period to verify that the variety is new, distinguishable, uniform, and stable.

Under Egyptian law, farmers are allowed to use, save, and replant seeds from protected varieties on their own land, subject to conditions set by the Minister of Agriculture. This is known as the "farmers' privilege" — an exception to the breeder's exclusive right.
