Legal Framework

What Is an Integrated Circuit?

Under Article 45 of Law No. 82 of 2002, an integrated circuit is defined as any product — in its final or intermediate form — containing components, at least one of which is an active element, mounted on an insulating substrate, and forming an integral unit designed to perform a specific electronic function.

Requirements for Protection

The layout design must be original — the result of the creator's own intellectual effort — and not commonplace in the semiconductor industry. Protection extends to original layout designs of integrated circuits under Article 46.

What Cannot Be Protected

Under Article 47, protection does not extend to any concept, process, system, technique, or coded information that may be embodied in the layout design. These remain outside the scope of protection.

Protection Term

10 years from the date of filing the registration application in Egypt, or from the date of first commercial exploitation — whichever is earlier. In all cases, protection expires 15 years from the date the design was created (Article 48).

Official Fees

The registration fee is determined by the Executive Regulations and shall not exceed EGP 1,000 per application. A register of applications is maintained at the Patent Office.

Registration Requirements

Under Article 49, the applicant must submit: a copy or drawing of the design, a sample of each integrated circuit commercially exploited, and information explaining the electronic function of the design.

Exclusive Rights

Under Article 50, the owner has the exclusive right to authorize or prohibit: reproduction of the protected layout design, importation, sale, and distribution of the design or products incorporating it.

What can be protected?

The protection covers the three-dimensional configuration of an integrated circuit — the arrangement of its elements and interconnections. It does not cover the concept, process, or technology behind the design, nor any coded information stored in it. The design must be original and not commonplace in the semiconductor industry.

Types of Layout Designs

A wide range of integrated circuit topographies can be protected in Egypt.

  • Microprocessors and central processing units
  • Memory chips (RAM, ROM, flash)
  • Application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs)
  • Field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs)
  • Analog and mixed-signal circuits
  • Power management integrated circuits
  • Radio frequency and wireless chips
  • Sensor and MEMS devices
  • System-on-chip (SoC) designs
  • Digital signal processors
  • Interface and connectivity chips
  • Custom and semi-custom integrated circuits

Common Questions About Integrated Circuits

A layout design (also called a topography) is the three-dimensional configuration of an integrated circuit — the arrangement of its elements and interconnections. It defines how the components of a chip are placed and connected to perform a specific electronic function. It is protected separately from the function itself.

Protection lasts for 10 years from the date of filing the registration application in Egypt, or from the date of first commercial exploitation — whichever is earlier. In all cases, protection expires 15 years from the date the design was created.

Under Article 49 of Law No. 82 of 2002, the application must include: a copy or drawing of the design; a sample of each integrated circuit that has been commercially exploited; and information explaining the electronic function of the design. The application is filed with the Egyptian Patent Office.

Under Article 50, the owner has the exclusive right to authorize or prohibit: reproduction of the protected layout design (in whole or in part); importation of the design or integrated circuits made from it; sale; and distribution of the design or products incorporating it.

Under Article 53, infringement of Article 50 is punishable by a fine of not less than EGP 20,000 and not more than EGP 100,000. In case of repeat offense, the penalty may include imprisonment. The court may also order seizure and destruction of infringing products.
