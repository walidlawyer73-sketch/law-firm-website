Egyptian Court System

The Judicial Structure

Egypt's civil and commercial courts operate on two levels of litigation — first instance and appeal — with cassation review before the Court of Cassation. Jurisdiction is primarily determined by the monetary value of the claim.

District Court (Mahkama Juz'iyya)

Composed of a single judge. Handles civil and commercial claims up to EGP 200,000 (following the 2024 amendments). Judgments are final — not subject to appeal — where the claim value does not exceed EGP 30,000.

Court of First Instance (Mahkama Ibtida'iyya)

Composed of a panel of judges. Handles claims exceeding EGP 200,000 and cases not within the District Court's jurisdiction. Judgments are final where the claim value does not exceed EGP 200,000.

Court of Appeal (Mahkama Isti'naf)

Reviews judgments of the Courts of First Instance where the claim value exceeds the finality threshold. Composed of a panel of three judges.

Court of Cassation (Mahkama Naqd)

The highest court in the ordinary judiciary. Reviews judgments for legal errors — cassation appeals are available where the claim value exceeds EGP 500,000 (per Law No. 157 of 2024).

Economic Courts

Specialized courts established by Law No. 120 of 2008 (as amended). Jurisdiction over economic disputes involving 21 specific laws — including intellectual property, competition, capital markets, and consumer protection. Primary circuits hear claims up to EGP 10 million.

State Council (Majlis al-Dawla)

Independent judicial body with exclusive jurisdiction over administrative disputes — including challenges to administrative decisions, government contracts, and disciplinary matters.

Note on the 2024 amendments

Under Law No. 157 of 2024 (effective 1 October 2024), the monetary jurisdiction thresholds for civil and commercial courts were increased: District Courts now handle claims up to EGP 200,000 (previously EGP 100,000); the finality threshold for District Court judgments is EGP 30,000 (previously EGP 15,000); and cassation appeals require a claim value exceeding EGP 500,000 (previously EGP 250,000).

Jurisdiction is primarily determined by the monetary value of the claim (for civil and commercial cases) and by subject matter (for specialized courts). Following the 2024 amendments, District Courts handle claims up to EGP 200,000, while Courts of First Instance handle claims exceeding that amount. Economic Courts have jurisdiction over specific economic matters regardless of value.

The ordinary courts (District Courts, Courts of First Instance, Courts of Appeal) handle the majority of civil and commercial disputes. The Economic Courts are specialized courts with jurisdiction over specific economic matters — including intellectual property, competition, capital markets, and consumer protection — as defined by Law No. 120 of 2008.

A cassation appeal to the Court of Cassation is available where the claim value exceeds EGP 500,000 (following the 2024 amendments). Cassation is not a re-trial — it reviews the judgment for legal errors, procedural defects, or misapplication of the law.

Timelines vary significantly depending on the court, complexity, and whether appeals are pursued.
